When you buy into a Palm Beach County country club community, one of the first things to find out is whether the club runs on an equity or non-equity membership model. It decides who owns the club, who votes on big decisions, and whether any of your initiation fee comes back to you when you sell.

Here's how the two models work, and the questions that tell you which one you're looking at.

## What is an equity country club?

In an equity club, the members own the club together. Your membership is a share of that ownership, which is why equity clubs usually give members voting rights on things like the budget, capital projects and assessments ([Real-ativity](https://www.real-ativity.com/?p=734)).

Equity clubs often return part of what you paid when you leave. How much, and when, is set by each club's documents. As one example, Woodfield Country Club in Boca Raton listed a full-member refund of 25% of the current standard equity price on the sale of the property, in its published schedule for November 1, 2024 through October 31, 2025 ([Woodfield equity and dues schedule, FY 2024–25](https://www.echofineproperties.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/Woodfield-Equity-and-Dues-FY-24-25-NEW-JOIN-FEES.pdf)). Terms change from year to year, so confirm the current schedule with the club.

## What is a non-equity country club?

In a non-equity club, you're buying access, not ownership. The club is owned by a developer, an operator or another organization, and the owner makes the decisions ([Real-ativity](https://www.real-ativity.com/?p=734)). The initiation fee is generally the price of admission and stays with the club, although some non-equity clubs refund a portion ([Private Club Marketing](https://privateclubmarketing.com/how-country-club-initiation-fees-work-equity-non-equity-refundable-deposits/)). If the value of memberships rises, that typically goes to the club or its owner rather than to the departing member ([PrivateCommunities.com](https://www.privatecommunities.com/blog/equity-vs-non-equity-golf.htm)).

## How do the two compare?

| | Equity club | Non-equity club |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns the club | The members, together | A developer, operator or organization |
| Voting on budgets and projects | Members usually vote | The owner decides |
| Initiation fee when you leave | Part may be refunded, per the club's documents | Usually kept by the club; some refund a portion |
| Dues after you resign | Some clubs bill dues until your spot is filled | Usually end when you leave |

The last row matters more than most buyers expect. At some equity clubs, a resigning member keeps paying dues until a new member takes the spot ([Real-ativity](https://www.real-ativity.com/?p=734)). Ask whether that applies, and how long the wait has been.

## Is "equity" always refundable?

No. The label alone doesn't tell you what comes back. Some clubs refund a certificate or equity portion on resale while the initiation portion is non-refundable, and some memberships are entirely non-refundable ([ActiveRain](https://activerain.com/blogsview/5947394/buying-into-a-south-florida-golf-community-2026-fees--memberships--and-what-to-verify)). Read the refund section of the membership documents, not just the brochure.

## What this means for buyers

The model shapes three things that a listing won't show you:

- **Your real cost to join and leave.** A higher initiation fee with a partial refund and a lower fee with no refund can land in very different places over the time you own the home.
- **Your say in the club.** At an equity club, members vote on assessments and capital projects. At a non-equity club, those decisions sit with the owner.
- **Your exit.** Refund timing, transfer rules and dues-until-replaced policies all affect how and when you can sell.

Many Palm Beach County club communities also tie membership to the home. Whether membership is mandatory, bundled or optional is a separate question, and we cover it in [mandatory vs. optional club membership](/journal/mandatory-vs-optional-club-membership/). It's also question two on our [Front Nine](/journal/the-front-nine/).

## Questions to ask the membership office

1. Is the club equity or non-equity, and who owns it?
2. What is the initiation or equity amount for the membership level I want, and for which year?
3. What portion is refunded when I leave, and when is it paid?
4. Do I owe dues after I resign, and until when?
5. Do members vote on assessments and capital projects?

## FAQ

### Do equity clubs cost more than non-equity clubs?

Not necessarily. Costs vary club by club and year by year. Compare the full picture, including initiation, any refund, annual dues, food and beverage minimums and assessments, using each club's current published schedule.

### Can a club change its refund terms?

Clubs can amend their governing documents, and Florida courts have heard disputes over changes to membership refund terms ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/node/62121/printable/print)). Ask how refund terms are set and whether they've changed for existing members.

### Is a non-equity club a worse deal?

It's a different structure. Non-equity can mean a lower or simpler cost to join and no dues after you leave, in exchange for no ownership or vote. Which fits depends on how long you plan to stay and how much say you want in the club.
