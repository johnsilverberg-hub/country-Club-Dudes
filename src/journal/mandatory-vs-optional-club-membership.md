In Palm Beach County club communities, the first membership question isn't the price. It's whether you have to join at all. Some communities require club membership with the home, some bundle it into what you pay as an owner, and others leave it up to you.

This one fact changes the full cost of the home, who your neighbors at the club are, and who can buy the home from you later.

## What does mandatory club membership mean?

In a mandatory-membership community, joining the club is a condition of owning a home there, whether or not you plan to use the amenities ([55places](https://www.55places.com/blog/golf-membership-included-in-hoa-fees)). The membership effectively runs with the home ([Million Luxury](https://www.millionluxury.com/news/mandatory-vs-optional-club-memberships-south-florida-luxury-buyers-guide)), so every owner is a member and pays the club's fees.

## What is a bundled golf community?

"Bundled" is the term you'll hear most for golf. In a bundled golf community, the golf membership comes with the home purchase, making every homeowner a member of the club ([PrivateCommunities.com](https://www.privatecommunities.com/blog/what-is-a-bundled-golf-community.htm)). Some bundled communities cover the course through the HOA dues; others bill a required membership separately, so non-golfers still pay for golf ([55places](https://www.55places.com/blog/golf-membership-included-in-hoa-fees)).

Some communities mix the two. At Mirasol in Palm Beach Gardens, for example, selected homes include a bundled or mandatory golf membership ([Roger Plevin](https://rogerplevin.com/blog/choosing-the-right-palm-beach-gardens-golf-community)). That's why it's worth asking about the specific home, not just the community.

## What does optional membership mean?

In an optional-membership community, you can buy the home without joining the club, and join later if you want to ([Daily Business Review](https://www.law.com/dailybusinessreview/2020/04/10/tough-decisions-for-hoas-facing-a-failing-golf-course/)). Membership may depend on availability, and some clubs keep a waitlist ([Million Luxury](https://www.millionluxury.com/news/mandatory-vs-optional-club-memberships-south-florida-luxury-buyers-guide)).

The Polo Club in Boca Raton is one example of an optional-membership community ([ListSellFL](https://listsellfl.com/blog/polo-club-boca-raton/)). PGA National in Palm Beach Gardens is resort-operated, with golf and non-golf membership tiers ([Roger Plevin](https://rogerplevin.com/blog/choosing-the-right-palm-beach-gardens-golf-community)).

## How do the three models compare?

| | Mandatory | Bundled | Optional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Do you have to join to buy? | Yes | Yes | No |
| Do you pay if you don't use it? | Yes | Yes | Only if you join |
| How it's billed | Club membership fees | Through HOA dues or a separate required membership | Club fees, only for members |
| Access | Every owner is a member | Every owner is a member | Can depend on availability or a waitlist |

## What else do you pay besides membership?

Membership is one line of several. Club communities can also carry neighborhood HOA or condo fees, Community Development District (CDD) fees, annual food and beverage minimums and capital or assessment charges ([Golf Life Navigators](https://golflifenavigators.com/?p=7040)). Compare homes by their total annual carrying cost and membership obligations, not by list price alone ([ActiveRain](https://activerain.com/blogsview/5947394/buying-into-a-south-florida-golf-community-2026-fees--memberships--and-what-to-verify)).

## What this means for buyers

- **If you'll use the club,** mandatory and bundled communities give every owner access, and the cost is part of owning the home. Ask how many members share the course and tee times, since every home is a member ([Golf Life Navigators](https://golflifenavigators.com/the-lowdown-on-bundled-golf-communities/)).
- **If you're not sure,** an optional community lets you buy the home first. Ask whether there's a waitlist and what joining later would cost.
- **For resale,** a mandatory membership means every future buyer has to join too. Factor that into how you think about selling.

Whether the club is equity or non-equity is a separate question; see [equity vs. non-equity country clubs](/journal/equity-vs-non-equity-country-clubs/).

## Questions to ask before you make an offer

1. Is membership mandatory with this specific home, bundled into the HOA, or optional?
2. If optional, is there a waitlist, and what would it cost to join later?
3. What are the HOA, CDD and any food and beverage minimums for this home?
4. Are any assessments approved or under discussion?
5. How does membership transfer when I sell?

## FAQ

### Can I live in a club community without golfing?

Yes. In an optional community you can skip the club entirely. In a mandatory or bundled community you'll pay for membership either way, and many clubs offer social or sports tiers for owners who don't golf. Ask which tiers satisfy the requirement.

### Where do I find out if membership is mandatory?

Confirm it in the HOA documents and the club's membership documents for the specific home ([Roger Plevin](https://rogerplevin.com/blog/choosing-the-right-palm-beach-gardens-golf-community)). Rules can differ between neighborhoods in the same community.

### Are optional-membership communities always cheaper?

Not always. Without the club, your annual cost can be lower, but HOA, CDD and other fees still apply, and joining later may cost more. Compare the full annual cost for the way you'd actually live there.
