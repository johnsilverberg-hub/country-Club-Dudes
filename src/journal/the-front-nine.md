Buying a home in a Palm Beach County club community is really two decisions: the house and the membership. Listing sites cover the first one. Almost none of them cover the second, and that's the one that shapes your costs, your social life and your resale for years.

We call these the Front Nine. Answer all nine for every club on your shortlist before you fall for a house.

## 1. Is membership mandatory with the home?

Some clubs require membership at closing. Others make it optional. It changes both what you'll pay each year and who your future buyers can be when you sell.

## 2. Equity or non-equity?

Equity means you own a share of the club. That affects refunds when you leave, how memberships transfer, and who votes on assessments.

## 3. What is the initiation or capital contribution, and is any of it refundable?

This is often a six-figure line item that sits completely outside the purchase price. Ask what it is for the year you'd join, what portion (if any) comes back to you, and when.

## 4. What do the annual dues cover?

Golf, sports and social tiers vary a lot from club to club, and so does what's included at each level. Compare the tier you'd actually use, not just the headline number.

## 5. Is there a food and beverage minimum?

Many clubs have a yearly spend you owe whether or not you eat at the club. Factor it into your real annual cost.

## 6. Are there any current or pending assessments?

Clubhouse and course projects get funded by members, sometimes right after a new member joins. Ask what's approved, what's being discussed, and how much each member pays.

## 7. How do tee times work in peak season?

Easy access in July can look very different in February. Ask how tee times are booked in season and how far ahead members book.

## 8. Can you rent before you buy, and do renters get club access?

Renting is the best way to test a club, if its rules allow it. Ask whether renters can use the club, and on what terms.

## 9. How long does membership approval take?

Applications and board reviews can add weeks to a normal closing timeline. Build that into your plans.
