Palm Beach County's 55+ communities range from ones with a full country club to ones with no club at all. "55+" isn't just a marketing label: it's a legal status with specific rules, and those rules affect who can live in the home, who can buy it from you later, and what the association will ask you for.

Here's how age-restricted communities work, based on the federal rules and Florida law.

## What makes a community "55+"?

Fair housing law generally prohibits discrimination based on familial status, meaning households with children. "Housing for older persons" is an exemption from that rule, and the 55-or-older version is the most common of the exemptions ([HUD](https://hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_housing_older_persons)).

To qualify, a community has to meet three requirements ([HUD](https://hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_housing_older_persons)):

1. **The 80% rule.** At least 80 percent of the occupied units must have at least one occupant who is 55 or older.
2. **Published policies.** The community must publish and follow policies and procedures that show it intends to operate as 55-or-older housing.
3. **Age verification.** The community must follow HUD's rules for verifying residents' ages.

Florida applies the same three requirements, citing both the federal rule and the Florida Statutes ([Florida Commission on Human Relations](https://fchr.myflorida.com/fchr55andolderhousing)).

## Does everyone in the home have to be 55?

No. The rule is about units, not people: at least one person in at least 80 percent of the occupied homes must be 55 or older ([HUD](https://hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_housing_older_persons)). That leaves room in the rules for some homes without a 55+ occupant. How a community uses that room is up to its own published policies, which can be stricter, so read them before you buy.

## How do communities verify ages?

The community must have procedures for regularly confirming who lives in each home, and those updates must take place at least once every two years, through surveys or other means ([eCFR, 24 CFR 100.307](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-24/subtitle-B/chapter-I/part-100/subpart-E/section-100.307)). One accepted form of verification is a signed certification from an adult in the household stating that at least one person in the home is 55 or older ([Cornell LII, 24 CFR 100.307](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.307)). A summary of the community's occupancy surveys must be available for inspection on reasonable request ([Cornell LII, 24 CFR 100.307](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/24/100.307)).

Expect the association to ask for age documentation when you buy, and periodically after that.

## Is "55+" the same as "62+"?

No. The law has a separate exemption for housing intended for, and solely occupied by, people 62 or older ([HUD](https://hud.gov/program_offices/fair_housing_equal_opp/fair_housing_act_housing_older_persons)). The 55+ exemption, with its 80% rule, is the more common of the two.

## Do 55+ communities have country clubs?

Some do and some don't. In Palm Beach County you'll find age-restricted communities with a full club and membership, and others with a clubhouse and amenities run by the homeowners association. If there's a club, the same membership questions apply as anywhere else: whether membership is [mandatory or optional](/journal/mandatory-vs-optional-club-membership/), and whether the club is [equity or non-equity](/journal/equity-vs-non-equity-country-clubs/).

## What this means for buyers

- **Read the age policy, not just the listing.** The community's published policies set its actual rules, including how it handles occupants under 55 and visitors.
- **Plan for verification.** Have age documentation ready, and expect follow-up surveys.
- **Think about resale.** The pool of future buyers follows the community's age policy.
- **Check the costs that come with it.** HOA fees, any club membership and assessments apply the same as in any community.

## Questions to ask the association

1. Is the community operated under the 55-or-older exemption, and can I see the published age policy?
2. What documentation do you need from buyers, and how often are age surveys done?
3. What are the rules for occupants and long-term guests under 55?
4. Can I rent the home, and do renters have to meet the age policy?
5. Is there a club, and is membership mandatory with the home?

## FAQ

### Can I buy in a 55+ community if I'm under 55?

It depends on the community. The 80% rule leaves room for some homes without a 55+ occupant, but each community's published policies decide how that room is used. Ask the association before you make an offer.

### Can a 55+ community stop being age-restricted?

Communities have to keep meeting the requirements to keep the exemption, including the 80% rule, published policies and age verification. Ask the association how it tracks compliance; its occupancy survey summary should be available on reasonable request.

### Are 55+ communities only in club communities?

No. Many 55+ communities in Palm Beach County have no country club membership at all. Browse [55+ communities](/search/55-plus/) to see both.
